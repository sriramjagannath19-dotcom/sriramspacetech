How a cold email got me into a national lab

2 min read · tap to open

I wanted to work on helium-3. It's a candidate fuel for future fusion power, and the only practical supply sits in lunar soil. But when I read about how you'd actually mine it, one problem kept coming up: lunar regolith is sharp, glassy dust, and it wears machines down fast.

That was a materials problem, and I didn't have a lab. So I wrote to one. I cold-emailed CSIR-National Metallurgical Laboratory and asked if I could work on it there.

They don't usually take high school students. They made an exception. I became one of the youngest researchers ever to work at CSIR-NML, working with scientists in the Advanced Materials & Coatings Division.

As primary inventor, I developed a new surface nanocoating for lunar hardware. We deposited it and wear-tested coated parts against uncoated ones. It cleared CSIR-NML's review and is now at CSIR Headquarters before patent filing.

What I took from it: the people who can help you are often one email away, and most never get asked. Write the email.
